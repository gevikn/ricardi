# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about the work, not about each other.

This project is about handing out a dangerous capability carefully, which means
the conversations worth having are often disagreements — about where a boundary
belongs, about whether a mitigation actually mitigates. Those are welcome, and
they go better when nobody is defending their ego along with their patch.

## What is expected

- Assume the other person read the thing and reached a different conclusion.
  Ask what they saw before explaining what they missed.
- Criticise the design, the code or the claim. Not the author.
- Say "I was wrong" plainly when you are, and move on without ceremony. It is
  the cheapest thing in engineering and the most useful.
- Accept that the maintainer may say no to work that is good, because the point
  of a small reviewable system is staying small and reviewable.
- If you report a security problem, you will be taken seriously and credited
  unless you ask not to be. See [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).

## What is not

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Sexualised language or attention.
Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained disruption of discussions.
Dismissing someone on the basis of who they are rather than what they argued —
including their experience level, which is not an argument.

None of this becomes acceptable because it was phrased as a joke, as bluntness,
or as being right.

## Scope

Anywhere this project is the subject: issues, pull requests, commit messages,
discussions, and private correspondence about it.

## Reporting

Report conduct problems the same way you would report a vulnerability — through
GitHub's private vulnerability reporting on this repository, which is a
confidential channel to the maintainer. Deliberately no email address is
published here; that is the same choice `SECURITY.md` makes.

Reports are read by the maintainer, who is also the person most likely to be the
subject of one. That is an honest limitation of a one-person project rather than
a policy: if the problem is the maintainer and a private channel to them is not
an acceptable route, say so publicly in an issue. That is a legitimate thing to
do here.

## Consequences

Depending on severity: a request to stop, having a comment removed, having a
pull request closed, or being blocked from the repository. There is no appeals
process to describe, because there is one maintainer — which is worth knowing
before you need it.

## Where this comes from
